# The Minimum Bar — checklist with inspection guidance

This is the checklist from *Prompt to Decision*, chapter 15 ("The Minimum
Bar"), verbatim in substance. It looks structurally like the other three
books' checklists, and it isn't — every single item here is a fact about
what somebody explicitly decided or stated, not a fact a file or a test
result can settle. Treating it like the other three checklists (go inspect
an artifact, report done/not-done) would silently misrepresent what this
skill can actually know, which is the one thing it must not do.

So every item below splits into exactly two questions, always, with no
exceptions the way the other books have `[checkable]` items:

- **Presence** — has this actually been stated somewhere, in writing, by
  someone? This much a search can settle: read the recommendation
  document, memo, or conversation the decision is based on, and find (or
  fail to find) an explicit statement of this element. Absence of an
  explicit statement is itself a real finding — "the objective is implied
  but never actually written down" is useful information, not a technicality.
- **Soundness** — assuming it was stated, is it *right*? Is this really the
  objective the affected people would recognize as theirs? Is this
  threshold one that will actually change the decision when crossed, or a
  number picked to look rigorous? Is this owner someone the consequences
  actually reach? This half is never something the skill resolves. It is
  a human judgment by construction — that's the entire argument of the
  book this checklist comes from — and the skill's job is to make sure the
  question gets asked pointedly, not to propose an answer, even a
  plausible-sounding one.

Report presence as a real finding. Report soundness as a question, always,
addressed to the human — never as a verdict.

**Telling "not found" apart from "ambiguous."** A memo is often full of
material that's adjacent to an item without actually satisfying it —
rationale, enthusiasm, a projected outcome — and it's tempting to let that
adjacency count as partial credit. It shouldn't. Ask specifically: does
this text name *this item's own subject* (an objective, a specific person,
a threshold), even loosely — or does it just happen to sit near where that
subject would go?

- If nothing in the material touches the item's actual subject at all —
  "Growth has been strong domestically" is rationale for expanding, not an
  objective statement, and doesn't name what's being optimized for — that's
  **not found**, not ambiguous. Don't let strong supporting narrative
  substitute for the specific thing being checked.
- If something does name the item's subject, but in a form too vague to
  actually act on — "the team" instead of a named owner, "leadership is
  supportive" instead of a stated risk tolerance — that's **ambiguous**.
  The subject was addressed; the answer just isn't specific enough to
  satisfy what the item needs (a person who can be asked, an objective
  concrete enough to disagree with).

When genuinely torn between the two, ambiguous is the safer default over
not-found only when there really is a sentence naming the right subject —
never because a "not found" verdict feels harsh to deliver.

## Getting the framing right

- **The objective has been stated explicitly, and the people it affects
  would recognise it as theirs.** Presence: is there an explicit sentence
  stating what this recommendation optimizes for, anywhere in the
  documents or conversation — not just implied by the recommendation
  itself? Soundness (ask): would the people this affects actually
  recognise that stated objective as theirs, or does it sound like
  something assumed on their behalf?
- **The alternatives considered include doing nothing, scored honestly.**
  Presence: is "continue as we are" named as an alternative anywhere, with
  an actual score or assessment attached — not just mentioned and
  dismissed in a clause? Soundness (ask): was it scored honestly, or
  assumed safe by default without the same scrutiny given to the
  recommended option?
- **Where objectives compete, the trade-off has been made visible.**
  Presence: is there an explicit statement of what's being traded against
  what — not just the winning objective, the shape of the actual trade-off?
  Soundness (ask): does that trade-off reflect the real curve, or only the
  one point on it that favors the recommendation?
- **Whose preferences and risk tolerance the recommendation was weighted
  against has been named explicitly.** Presence: does any document say,
  by name or role, whose preferences set the weighting? Soundness (ask):
  is that actually the right person or group, or did the weighting default
  to whoever built the recommendation?

## Not fooling yourself

- **A specific, measurable threshold — with a date and a consequence —
  has been set for what would count as this being wrong.** Presence: is
  there a stated number, a stated date, and a stated consequence if it's
  crossed — all three, not just a vague "we'll keep an eye on it"?
  Soundness (ask): is that threshold one that would genuinely change the
  decision if crossed, or a number chosen because it looked unlikely to be
  hit?
- **The commitment has been split into reversible and irreversible parts,
  held to different bars.** Presence: does anything distinguish which
  parts of this commitment can be undone from which can't? Soundness
  (ask): is the irreversible part actually getting the higher scrutiny, or
  is it riding along on the reversible part's lighter justification?
- **The downside has been sized concretely, not just as a probability.**
  Presence: is there a concrete description of what the worst realistic
  case actually looks like to live through — not just "X% chance of
  failure"? Soundness (ask): does that description feel real to the people
  who'd live through it, or does it read like a risk-register entry nobody
  actually pictured?
- **What waiting, or a smaller test, would teach has been asked before
  committing to the full-scale version.** Presence: is there any mention
  of what a delay or a smaller pilot would reveal, and why that wasn't
  chosen instead? Soundness (ask): is that reasoning genuine, or a
  justification for a decision already made on other grounds?

## Knowing what you're actually committing to

- **Second-order effects have been named, even qualitatively.** Presence:
  does anything name a consequence that follows from the direct result,
  rather than the decision itself (capacity, attention, quality for people
  not part of the plan)? Soundness (ask): is that list actually complete,
  or does it stop at the first one that was easy to think of?
- **Who bears the risk if this goes wrong is known, and it isn't only the
  people most insulated from it who are pushing for it.** Presence: does
  anything name who specifically bears the cost if this fails? Soundness
  (ask): is there a mismatch between who's pushing for this and who
  absorbs it if it's wrong?
- **The recommendation has survived a real challenge.** Presence: is there
  any record of actual pushback — a specific objection raised and
  addressed — or does the record show only the first version, unchallenged?
  Soundness (ask): was the challenge real, or a formality nobody actually
  pressed on?

## Before you actually act

- **One person or a small, named group owns this decision, with authority
  to act on the threshold.** Presence: is a specific name or small named
  group attached to this decision anywhere, as opposed to "the team" or
  "we"? Soundness (ask): does that owner actually have the authority to
  act when the threshold is crossed, without re-litigating the whole
  decision?
- **That owner is someone the consequences genuinely reach.** Presence:
  is there anything establishing the owner's relationship to the outcome?
  Soundness (ask): is this person actually exposed to what happens if it
  goes wrong, or insulated enough that acting on bad news costs them
  little?

## The short version (five highest-value items)

For a lighter check rather than a full pre-decision review — presence and
the human question, for each:

1. Is the objective stated explicitly, and would the affected people
   recognise it as theirs?
2. Was doing nothing scored honestly, not just mentioned and dismissed?
3. Is there a specific, dated threshold with a stated consequence?
4. Is the irreversible part of this held to a higher bar than the
   reversible part?
5. Does a specific, named person own this, with real authority and real
   exposure to the outcome?

Every one of these is still two questions, not one — presence first,
soundness always handed back to the human.
