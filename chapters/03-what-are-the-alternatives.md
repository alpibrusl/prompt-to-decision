# What Are the Alternatives?

Ask the agent to compare expanding into Germany versus France, and it will tell you, honestly and well, which one wins. That answer can be completely correct and still be the wrong recommendation to act on, because the real best option was never one of the two it was asked to compare.

## Optimizing is only as good as the menu

An **alternative** is one of the specific options actually being weighed against each other in a recommendation. "Germany or France" is a comparison between two alternatives. It is not the same thing as "every option Ledgerly actually has," and the gap between those two is where a great many bad decisions quietly originate — not from choosing badly among the options considered, but from never considering the option that mattered most.

An agent optimizes over whatever alternatives it was handed, or whatever alternatives seemed implied by the request. Ask "should we expand into Germany or France" and you will get a careful, honest comparison of exactly those two things — not a challenge to the premise that expansion is the right category of move at all. The agent didn't fail to think broadly; it answered the comparison you actually posed, which is a narrower job than widening it, and widening it was never asked for.

## The alternatives that get left off, and why

A few kinds of option are systematically likely to go missing from a comparison, not because anyone hid them but because they're not what naturally comes to mind when a request is already framed around expansion.

**Doing nothing.** Covered in full in the next chapter, because it deserves its own — but it is the single most commonly missing alternative in any comparison, and its absence is so routine it's easy to stop noticing.

**A smaller, cheaper version of the same idea.** "Expand into Germany with six hires" and "test Germany with one person and a local partnership for two quarters first" are different alternatives, and the second one frequently never makes it onto the list, because the request was already framed at the scale of the bigger version.

**A completely different use of the same resources.** Six hires and a Germany office is one way to spend the runway a seed round bought. Doubling down on the product feature that already tested well, in the market Ledgerly already understands, is another. These compete for the same limited resource — time, cash, founder attention — and a comparison scoped to "which country" never puts them in the same frame at all.

## Widening the comparison is cheap; narrowing it silently is not

The instinct to resist here is treating a wider comparison as more expensive to produce. It usually isn't. Asking an agent to also score "stay domestic and reinvest in the feature that's working" alongside "Germany" and "France" costs almost nothing extra — the same evidence, the same model, one more column in the same table. What's expensive is discovering eight months into a Germany expansion that the domestic option was actually stronger, after the resources that would have funded it are already spent.

The habit worth building: before accepting any comparison as complete, ask what's not on the list, and specifically ask about the two categories above — a cheaper trial of the same idea, and an entirely different use of the same resources. If the honest answer is "we didn't consider those," that's not necessarily wrong — sometimes the obvious options really are the only serious ones — but it should be a decision made on purpose, not an accident of how the first question got phrased.

## What to ask for

> "What alternatives did you actually compare, and what are the two or three most plausible options you didn't include? Score those too."

The default request. A comparison that can't name what it left out is a comparison nobody has actually checked the boundaries of.

> "Compare this option against a smaller, cheaper version of the same idea — a pilot or a trial — not just against the other full-scale alternatives."

Forces the scaled-down option into the frame, which Chapter 10 will return to as a discipline in its own right.

> "What would we do with the same time and money if we didn't do any of these? Score that as a real alternative, not as a footnote."

The domestic-reinvestment question, generalised — the option that competes for the same resources but was never in the original comparison because it wasn't what the request was about.
