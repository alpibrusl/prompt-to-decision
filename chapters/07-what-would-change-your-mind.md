# What Would Change Your Mind?

Ledgerly's founders decide to expand into Germany. Three months in, German revenue is modest. Is that early-stage normal, or is it the plan failing? Nobody can say, because nobody decided in advance what "failing" would actually look like.

## A decision without a threshold cannot be wrong

A **decision threshold** is a specific, stated condition, set before acting, that would mean a decision was a mistake — not a vague sense that things aren't going great, but a number or an event named in advance that everyone involved agreed would count as a signal to stop, change course, or pull back. "If German revenue hasn't reached €15,000 monthly recurring by month four, we pause and reassess" is a decision threshold. "We'll know it if we see it" is not one, and it is the default that fills the space whenever a real one doesn't get set.

Without a stated threshold, a struggling plan and a plan that's on a normal, slower-than-hoped trajectory look identical from the inside, for as long as anyone wants them to. Every disappointing month can be explained as early-stage normal, because nothing was ever defined as the alternative. This is not usually dishonesty. It's what happens by default when optimism is cheap and nobody paid the small upfront cost of writing down what would count as being wrong.

## Setting the threshold after you've already started doesn't work

The reason this has to happen before acting, not once results start coming in, is that a threshold set afterward gets quietly negotiated by the results themselves. Three months into a disappointing Germany expansion, "we said we'd reassess at €15,000 monthly" is a hard, external commitment made by a version of you that didn't yet know how it would feel to consider pulling back. "Let's see how it's really going" three months in, with no prior number, is a judgment made by the version of you that's already emotionally and financially invested in it working — sunk cost, personal identity tied up in the decision, the natural and very human pull toward finding a reason to keep going a little longer.

This is exactly why the threshold belongs in Chapter 7, sized before Chapters 8 and 9 ask how reversible the decision is and what being wrong would actually cost. A threshold set before acting is a commitment to your future self about what evidence would count. A threshold negotiated after the fact usually isn't a threshold at all — it's a story about why the current results are actually fine.

## What a good threshold actually looks like

A useful threshold has three things a vague one doesn't: a specific number or event, a specific date by which it applies, and it has to be something you would actually notice and act on, not something so extreme it would never plausibly be reached even in a genuine failure.

"If it's not working we'll know" fails all three. "If German MRR is below €15,000 at month four, we stop hiring further into that market and reassess with the board" passes them — it names the number, the date, and the specific action that follows, which means the four-month mark actually functions as a decision point instead of just another month on the calendar.

## What to ask for

> "Before we commit to this, what specific, measurable outcome — by what date — would tell us this isn't working? Name it now, not after we've started."

The core request. If nobody can name a number, that's the finding: the plan currently has no way to fail, which also means it has no way to succeed in any checkable sense.

> "What action follows automatically if we hit that threshold? Not 'we'll discuss it' — what actually happens?"

A threshold with no attached action is a number nobody's committed to acting on, which functions the same as not having one.

> "Are we about to explain away a result that would have counted as the threshold, if we'd actually set one? Check the number we would have used, honestly, against what's happening now."

Worth asking mid-flight, on any decision that's already underway without a threshold having been set — the honest retroactive version, better late than never.
